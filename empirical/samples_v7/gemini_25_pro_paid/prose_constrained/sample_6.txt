There is a peculiar peace to be found within the sterile, sprawling corridors of an airport, a comfort that defies all logic. By any rational measure, these are places of stress and anxiety, of frantic schedules, bureaucratic hurdles, and the impersonal churn of mass transit. They are non-places, architectural voids designed for the sole purpose of moving people from one real place to another. Yet, it is precisely within this liminality, this state of being neither here nor there, that a strange and profound sense of tranquility can descend. It is the comfort of the pause button, a moment of suspended animation in the relentless narrative of our lives. When you step through the sliding glass doors into the terminal, you shed your immediate world like a heavy coat. The responsibilities of home and the demands of your destination are held at bay, separated from you by the vast, humming space of the concourse. Your only duty is to wait, to follow the simple, linear instructions of screens and signs.

This suspension of self is a quiet liberation. In the outside world, we are defined by our roles: employee, parent, partner, neighbor. We carry the weight of these identities, the expectations and histories that come with them. But in an airport, you are anonymous. You become a passenger, a seat number, a destination on a boarding pass. The thousands of strangers flowing around you know nothing of your triumphs or your failures, your joys or your sorrows.
